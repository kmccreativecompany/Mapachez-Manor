Silver Key Hospitality

Gamified hospitality training for people who learn by doing.

Would your employees rather play games and watch short videos than read another training manual? Good. Silver Key turns hospitality training into realistic, repeatable play: guests, records, service recovery, inspection clues, costs, handoffs, and emotional judgment.

Employees do not just read hospitality standards. They experience them through playable situations before they matter in front of a real guest.
Core productMulti-role hospitality simulation, not a static manual and not a one-property game.
Flagship worldMapáchez Manor remains the cozy luxury resort world where the first modules live.
Training promisePractice judgment, service language, system fluency, cost awareness, and guest comfort in one place.
For hotel ownersShow new hires what good decisions look like before the busy shift begins.
For managersUse scenarios to coach consistency, handoffs, labor awareness, and recovery without shaming staff.
For employeesLearn through role play, repetition, visual records, and calm feedback instead of memorizing walls of text.

Service Steps

Make the wine list feel human.

The wine list should not require a minor in ancient riddles. Confirm the guest's goal, speak in plain flavor words, and document what mattered.

1Verify age/service policy, occasion, food pairing, allergy/dietary flags, and budget comfort.
2Offer two clear options with flavor words: crisp, soft, dry, sweet, bright, cozy.
3Confirm glass or bottle before opening or posting anything.
4Present the bottle label, open calmly, pour according to policy, and check back after the first sip.
5Record a useful preference only if it will help later: "liked crisp citrus whites," not "complicated."

Night Desk Training

Overnight work, organized for a tired brain.

This module borrows the useful training shape from the overnight hub and rebuilds it as generic hotel practice: no real property names, vendor names, contacts, passwords, room codes, or branded workflows.

How To Use This In Play

When the player is on an overnight scenario, they should move in this order: stabilize the guest, check the record, make the smallest accurate promise, document the next action, and protect the morning handoff.

1Take HandoffRead open promises, room issues, late arrivals, and anything waiting on another team.
2Open ToolsUse the Hotel Management System, guest messaging, work orders, activities, and internal communication tools.
3Check RecordsSearch by name or room, then confirm stay status, room readiness, notes, and assigned tasks.
4Watch AuditLook for late arrivals, no-shows, open folios, routing issues, and room status blockers.
5Handoff CleanlySend objective open items with owner, next action, timing, and urgency.

Start ShiftEquipment, briefing, and system readiness
  • Collect required shift equipment according to local policy, such as keys, radio, device, or cash drawer.
  • Receive the outgoing team's briefing before taking new work.
  • Open the generic tools needed for the night: Hotel Management System, guest messaging, work orders, activities or dining, transportation requests, and internal communication.
  • Check unresolved guest promises before starting new tasks.
Active Work TrayHold and resume interrupted tasks
  • Open one task card when work is interrupted, then return to it when the guest-facing moment is stable.
  • Each active task needs an owner, next action, deadline, and current status.
  • Close completed tasks so the board stays readable.
  • Move unfinished tasks into the official shift handoff instead of trusting memory.
Standard Card HabitProcedure help without a giant manual
  • Use a standard card when the player asks, "What is the normal way to do this?"
  • Keep each card short: trigger, standard, steps, documentation, and escalation.
  • Generic examples belong here; real passwords, vendor links, contacts, and property codes do not.
  • If the procedure is unclear, create an open item instead of inventing a confident answer.
Stuck At NightFast help when the desk is quiet but urgent
  • Stabilize the guest first: acknowledge, set a short update window, and avoid overpromising.
  • Search the Hotel Management System by name or room before making any service, billing, or room-status promise.
  • Use a quick phrase if you need time: "I am checking the current record so I can answer accurately."
  • If the answer is still uncertain, document the blocker and assign the next owner.
Arrivals And Room ReadinessName search, room search, profile check
  • Search by guest name, room number, record number, or room type before creating new work.
  • Sort records alphabetically when looking for people; sort by room when organizing packets or room-specific work.
  • Check room status before promising access, luggage delivery, amenities, or early arrival readiness.
  • Verify celebrations, dietary restrictions, allergies, animal guest notes, accessibility needs, and room preferences before staging anything.
Guest CommunicationShort, accurate, and calm
  • Acknowledge first, then check the record before promising.
  • Use a template only as a starting point; personalize only with details the guest has actually shared.
  • Choose the channel by urgency: call for urgent or sensitive matters, message for simple updates, email for longer pre-arrival details.
  • When delayed, give the next update time instead of letting the wait become mysterious.
Night Audit WatchEnd-of-day blockers
  • Review late remaining arrivals and document guests who will arrive after the normal window.
  • Track no-show review items, reinstated stays, and any reservation changes that affect the next business date.
  • Check open folios, routing instructions, group billing, payment status, and unresolved charges before the date rolls.
  • If something blocks audit, record the issue, the owner, the action taken, and who needs to see it in the morning.
In-Room Dining and Morning SetupBreakfast, allergy checks, and service reset
  • Confirm dietary restrictions and allergies in both the guest record and the order notes.
  • Prepare trays or tables with polished serviceware, clean linens, correct condiments, and a realistic delivery quote.
  • Log orders that need pickup, follow-up, or special timing.
  • Before morning coverage begins, reset supplies, coffee or tea station basics, and open service notes.
Cash And Folio ControlNo guessing, no casual fixes
  • Count and reconcile the drawer or payment materials according to hotel policy.
  • Do not guess on overages, shortages, deposits, charge transfers, or direct-bill questions.
  • Use the folio and routing rules before moving charges or discussing balances with the guest.
  • Escalate variance or payment uncertainty through the approved manager path.
Clean HandoffObjective notes for the next shift
  • Use topic buckets: arrivals, room issues, guest promises, billing watch, assigned tasks, and manager follow-up.
  • Write what happened, what is next, who owns it, when it is due, and how urgent it is.
  • Keep informal notes free of payment details, private medical details, and unnecessary personal judgments.
  • Use respectful service language: specific, factual, and useful to the next associate.
Late ArrivalA guest messages after midnight. The player must verify the record, protect room readiness, and communicate calmly.
Breakfast OrderAn early order includes a dietary note. The player must confirm the profile, setup details, timing, and pickup follow-up.
Room IssueA guest reports a maintenance concern. The player must create a work order, offer a service path, and assign morning ownership.
Open ItemA task survives the night. The player must write a handoff that another associate can act on without re-interviewing the guest.

Learning Ledger

Labor, supplies, capital, gains, and tax questions.

Supply CostRecurring items such as linens, amenities, cleaning goods, repairs, and inventory.
CapitalMoney tied up in purchase, durable improvements, financing, reserves, or setup.
